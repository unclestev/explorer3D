A "wheel" = a non-standard-mapping gamepad with ≥3 axes, or an id matching wheel/G29/G920/G923/G27/Driving Force/racing/logitech; G29-like ids preferred. Steering = axis 0, deadzone 1.5%, linear: full wheel (G29 900°) = full `steerLock` — about a real car's ratio. Pedal axes differ by browser/driver/mode switch, so they're **learned once per device id**: ⚙️ **Wheel** row (`#wheelRow`, hidden until a wheel is seen, status `#wheelStat`: Not connected / Pedals not set up / Setting up… / Ready) → "Set up pedals" (`#wheelBtn` → `WHEEL.setup()`): press gas fully + let go, then brake. Saved in localStorage `ydWheel` {id: {gas:{i,rest,full}, brake:{…}}}; value = clamp((raw−rest)/(full−rest)), < .03 → 0; gas = gas − brake (brake at a stop = reverse, as with the touch pedals; works on foot and in the glider too). While setting up, read() returns st 0 / gas 0 so the car doesn't move. **Chrome reports an untouched pedal axis as exactly 0 until it first moves**, so each pedal axis is ignored until it has read non-zero (else a resting G29 pedal would read half pressed); reset on reconnect. Toasts: "🏁 Wheel connected" (+ "set up the pedals in ⚙️" if not learned), "Wheel disconnected". **No force feedback** — browsers can't drive wheel FFB, so the G29 only self-centres if G HUB's centering spring is turned on. No buttons mapped yet (keyboard F/G/Space still work). - **Top speed (game.js step()):** with `CARSPEC.vmax` set (Javelin: 120/2.237 m/s) and in car mode going forward, drive accel = gas·12·(1−r²) + min(1, 2·gas)·drag(vmax)·r², r = v/(1.012·vmax), and speed is clamped at vmax. ## Known limits
- Traffic cars don't collide with you (you pass through them — on foot too), don't yield at uncontrolled intersections, and ignore hills (flat ground height).
- Stop-sign / red-light penalties only count signs and lights within a few metres of your actual path (cross-street signs beside the road are ignored).
- Weather is model-based (Open-Meteo), not radar: very light or very local showers can still be missed.
- Satellite photos are a flat drape: trees and buildings in the photo are "painted on" the ground next to the 3D ones, and imagery dates vary.
- Water-tower lettering is built from small coloured blocks, so it looks slightly pixelated up close; the tank outline shows faint ring steps. Which way the logo faces is random per tower, not the real orientation. The Sugar Grove letters use Georgia (on iPhone); other phones fall back to another serif.
- Tower beacons use MapLibre internals (`map.transform`, `pixelMatrix3D`, `map.terrain`) for the 3D position; if a MapLibre upgrade changes them, the dots will be misplaced — check them first after any upgrade. Unincorporated towers just outside the town limits (e.g. Boulder Hill) keep the generic model unless their OSM name/operator/city says Oswego, Yorkville or Sugar Grove.
- The ETA assumes driving at the speed limits (OSRM's times); it doesn't adapt to how fast you're actually going.
- The tractor sound is a modern 6R-style PowerTech six (matching the cab tractor model), not the classic two-cylinder "Johnny Popper"; the popper was offered as an alternative.
- Engine sounds are syntheses, not recordings; they capture each engine's rhythm (firing order, cylinder count, turbo behaviour) but won't match a specific car exactly. Need AudioWorklet (iOS 14.5+); the iPhone silent switch mutes them. The worklet runs continuously while driving with sound on, which costs some battery — ⚙️ Sound off saves it.
- All cars accelerate the same way (same 0–60); only the Javelin's top speed differs (120 mph vs the Explorer's and Navigator's ~94 mph at full pedal / ~80 at a normal press, from the old formula — the real ST is governed at ~143 mph). The Navigator's engine sound reuses the Explorer's EcoBoost synth (different gearing and rpm, same voice). The owner was offered a real Explorer top speed / quicker ST acceleration; not done yet.
- The Javelin cut-out has a few small notches along the body sides where bright reflections met the white background.
- Reopening the app restores the position but not the mode on foot (you restart in the car at its parked spot), any route, challenge or speed. A saved spot right after a crash or inside a building's footprint is restored as is.
- Look around: the photo cars are pictures, not models. From behind and the sides they're a billboard of the rear photo; from the front the Explorer shows one fixed front three-quarter photo (the same angle whether you look from 110° or straight ahead; mirrored on the passenger side, so the Ford badge and "ST" read backwards there), and the Javelin/Navigator show the generic 3D SUV in the car's colour. The switch between rear and front photo is a hard cut at ~100°. The Explorer front photo is a different black than the rear photo's grey-blue ST, and it has no headlight glow at night. With the view panned you still steer relative to the vehicle, not the screen.
- Pitched roofs are guesses (map data rarely has roof shape) and show small steps up close; houses split across map tiles or mapped as several parts keep flat roofs. Only houses near the car get pitched roofs; far ones show just the coloured roof cap.
- Windows and doors are guesses at real positions (map data doesn't have them); attached townhouses may get windows on shared walls. Only the nearest ~150 buildings (80 in saver) get them; lit windows are just a warm colour (no light cast). They remain the most expensive visual feature; if battery is still an issue, the next step is fewer facade buildings (or none) in battery saver — ask the owner first.
- Trees avoid building footprints only where the building is in the loaded map tiles; trees can still stand on roads/driveways inside a wood or park polygon.
- 3D street signs: oversized (~10× a real blade) so they can be read; the blade for the road you're on is seen nearly edge-on from straight behind (realistic, but you only read it from an angle or after turning). Long names are squeezed rather than lengthened. They sit on flat ground like the traffic cars (on hills they can float or sink a little), and the car layer draws over map 3D buildings except where the building check hides the whole sign. Which corner gets the post is fixed per intersection, not chosen by the approach direction. Checked in a headless three.js render, not yet on the phone.
- Javelin 3D / 3D v2: game-ready models (2,652 / 2,868 triangles), so smoother/simpler than the photo up close; the gold stripe is on the hood only as the model has it. v2 is the reworked file (arches, liners, glass, trim — see `models/`); its arch panels are flat-shaded, so faint lines show where they meet the original panels at z ±0.85. No headlight beam position check yet (lamp .5 / front 2.67 from the model's quad headlights). Records, sounds and top speed are shared with the photo Javelin.
- On foot: the person is a simple original figure (no photo), no door animation or footstep sounds; from the front the parked Explorer shows its front photo, while the Javelin and Navigator still show the generic 3D SUV in roughly their colour (a Javelin then looks like an SUV). No getting out in the boat. Walking runs at the game's 3× world speed, so a walk covers ground faster than real life. Checked only in a stubbed headless harness; the live check is on the owner's phone.
- Wheel: no force feedback (browser limit), no buttons/paddles/shifter/clutch used yet, combined-pedals mode (gas and brake on one axis) isn't supported by the setup (switch it off in the driver). Only the first wheel found is used. Steering lock still shrinks with speed (`steerLock`), so the same wheel angle turns less at speed. Not yet tried on a real G29.
